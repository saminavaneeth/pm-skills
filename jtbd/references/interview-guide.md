# JTBD Interview Guide

Use this guide to produce a **tailored** guide for the PM's job and segment. Replace the placeholders with the specific job, solutions, and circumstances. Two interview types:

- **Switch interview**: reconstructs a recent purchase or switch decision, to uncover the forces of progress and the trigger events. Use it when the question is *why people adopt or switch*.
- **Job-map interview**: walks through the last time the person did the job, to uncover the steps, struggles, and desired outcomes. Use it when the question is *what to improve*.

The two can be combined in one 45–60 minute session: switch timeline first, job walkthrough second.

## Rules for the interviewer

- **Ask about specific past events, never hypotheticals.** Say "Tell me about the last time…", not "Would you…?" or "Do you usually…?"
- **Never pitch, demo, or defend the product.** You are a documentary maker, not a salesperson.
- **Get concrete detail**: dates, places, people, what was on screen, what they did next. Detail jogs memory and exposes the real story.
- **Dig into "why then?"** The moment of switching or starting reveals the push.
- **Follow energy.** When they sigh, laugh, or say "ugh", ask "What happened there?"
- **Don't accept "it's easier" or "it's better".** Ask "Easier how? Compared to what? What did that let you do?"
- **Silence is a tool.** Wait 3 seconds after they answer before you speak.
- **Avoid** leading questions, feature wish lists ("What would you like us to build?"), and ratings without stories.

## Recruiting

Interview people who **recently made progress, or tried to**. Their memory is fresh.

- **Switchers**: started using, or switched to, a solution for <job> in the **last 90 days**. Include people who chose a competitor.
- **Leavers**: stopped using a solution for <job> in the last 90 days.
- **Strugglers / non-consumers**: do <job> with workarounds or don't do it at all, despite the need. Ask about **the last time they needed to and didn't** ("the last thing you saw and didn't report"), not only the last time they did. Non-consumption leaves no record, so this is often the only evidence of it.

Sample screener:
1. In the last 3 months, have you started, changed, or stopped how you <do the job>? (Started / Changed / Stopped / None → end)
2. What were you using before, and what do you use now? (open)
3. Were you involved in choosing it? (Yes, I decided / I influenced it / No → end)
4. <segment qualifier, e.g. company size, role>

Aim for **8–12 interviews per segment**. Stop when new interviews stop revealing new forces or job steps.

## Part A: Switch interview (25–30 min)

Build the **timeline** from first thought to ongoing use. Work backwards from the purchase if that's easier for them.

| Phase | Prompts |
|---|---|
| **Set the scene** | "When did you start using <solution>? What day was it? Where were you?" |
| **First thought** | "When did you first realize the old way wasn't working? What happened that day?" (**push**, **trigger**) |
| **Passive looking** | "Between that moment and actively looking, what did you notice or try? How long was that?" |
| **Event → active looking** | "What made you finally start looking for real? Why then and not a month earlier?" (**trigger**) |
| **Options considered** | "What else did you look at, including doing nothing or doing it yourself? What did each promise?" (**pull**, **competing solutions**) |
| **Hesitations** | "What almost stopped you? What were you worried could go wrong? Who else had to agree?" (**anxiety**) |
| **What you'd miss** | "What did you like about the old way? What was hard to give up?" (**habit**) |
| **Deciding** | "What tipped it? What did you have to see or hear to say yes?" |
| **First use** | "Tell me about the first time you used it. What did you expect? What happened?" |
| **Ongoing use** | "What's it doing for you now that the old way didn't? Is anything worse?" (**fired because / hired because**) |

## Part B: Job-map interview (20–30 min)

Anchor on **the most recent time** they did <job>.

1. "Walk me through the last time you <did the job>, from the moment you knew you had to do it until it was done."
2. For each step they mention:
   - "What were you trying to achieve in that step?" (**step**)
   - "How did you know you'd done it well?" (**desired outcome / metric**)
   - "What took the most time? What could go wrong? What did go wrong?" (**struggles**)
   - "What did you use: tools, people, notes?" (**current solutions**)
3. Probe the universal steps they skipped:
   - **Define**: "How did you decide what needed to be done?"
   - **Locate**: "What did you need to gather first? From where?"
   - **Confirm**: "How did you check you were ready to start?"
   - **Monitor**: "How did you know it was going well or badly?"
   - **Modify**: "When something was off, what did you do?"
   - **Conclude**: "How did you know you were finished? What happened after?"
4. "Was that a typical time? What's different when <circumstance>?" (**circumstances**)
5. "How did you feel at the end? What would your <manager / client> think if it went badly?" (**emotional and social jobs**)
